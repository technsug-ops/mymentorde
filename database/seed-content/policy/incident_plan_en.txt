1. Definition

A "personal data breach" within the meaning of Art. 4(12) GDPR is a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to personal data.

Examples:
- Unauthorized access to the database (hacking, insider)
- Loss of an unencrypted employee laptop containing client data
- Accidentally sending an email with client data to the wrong recipient
- Successful phishing attack: employee credentials compromised
- Hosting provider incident (e.g., data leak at ALL-INKL.COM)
- Stripe account takeover or payment data leak
- Misconfigured permissions resulting in cross-tenant data access
- Loss of a mobile device with access to internal back-office tools

3. Detection and Initial Reporting

3.1 Detection Sources
- Automated alerts: audit log anomalies, brute-force detection, hosting provider notifications, Stripe security emails.
- Manual indicators: employee observations, client complaints, external researcher reports.
- Third-party incidents: AVV partners (Resend, PostHog, AI providers, Stripe) must inform us of their own incidents within 48 hours.

3.2 Initial Internal Report
For every suspected case, a report MUST be made within 1 hour to:
- Email: info@mentorde.com (management)
- Phone: +49 157 84218282

Required information:
- When and how was the incident detected?
- Which systems / data are (presumably) affected?
- Is the incident ongoing or contained?
- Who reported the incident?

4. Containment (Immediate Measures, T+0 to T+4h)

Immediately after the initial report, the following measures must be evaluated and initiated as needed:

- Isolate affected systems (e.g., take server off the network, lock compromised accounts).
- Reset passwords for admin accounts, invalidate sessions.
- Renew 2FA tokens for affected accounts.
- Copy backups to separate storage (evidence preservation).
- Protect audit logs from tampering (read-only snapshot).
- For third-party incidents: rotate API keys (Stripe, Resend, AI providers), invalidate webhook signatures.

5. Assessment (T+1h to T+24h)

5.1 Risk Assessment under Art. 33 GDPR

Evaluate the following factors:

| Criterion | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number of affected persons | < 10 | 10-1,000 | > 1,000 |
| Data sensitivity | Pseudonyms | Contact data | Passport data, health, payment data |
| Identifiability | Anonymized | Pseudonymized | Directly identifiable |
| Likelihood of misuse | Very low | Possible | Actively misused |
| Damage potential | Reputation | Harassment | Identity theft, financial damage |

If assessment shows "likely risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons" → notification to supervisory authority is required (Art. 33 GDPR).

If assessment shows "likely HIGH risk" → additional notification of data subjects required (Art. 34 GDPR).

5.2 Documentation
Even for incidents that are not reportable (e.g., internal audit findings without data leak), an entry is created in the internal incident register — regardless of the risk level (Art. 33(5) GDPR).

6. Notification to Supervisory Authority (T+24h to T+72h)

If reportable: notification MUST occur within 72 hours of becoming aware of the incident (Art. 33(1) GDPR). Late notifications must include reasoning.

Content of notification (Art. 33(3) GDPR):
- Description of the nature of the breach (categories of affected data and persons, approximate number of subjects).
- Name and contact details of the data protection contact (info@mentorde.com).
- Likely consequences of the incident.
- Measures already taken or proposed.

If information is incomplete, the notification can be supplemented progressively (Art. 33(4) GDPR).

7. Notification of Data Subjects (Art. 34 GDPR)

In case of likely HIGH risk, the data subjects must be informed without undue delay — in a language they can understand (Turkish / German / English depending on user's language).

Exceptions (Art. 34(3) GDPR):
- Data was appropriately encrypted (encryption effective against unauthorized parties).
- Subsequent measures taken that exclude the high risk.
- Disproportionate effort → public announcement instead of individual notification.

Content of data subject notification:
- Description of the nature of the breach (in plain language).
- Name and contact details of the data protection contact.
- Likely consequences.
- Recommendations for risk mitigation (e.g., change password, block bank card).
- Measures taken and planned.

Distribution channel: Resend transactional email to all affected. For very large incidents, possibly additional public announcement at mentorde.com/incident.

8. Recovery and Remediation (T+24h to T+30 days)

- Clean systems (remove malware, close backdoors).
- Restore backups (in case of data loss).
- Close security gaps (patches, harden configurations).
- Renew compromised credentials (Stripe API key, Resend API key, AI API keys, admin passwords).
- If needed: external forensic analysis.

9. Post-Incident Review (T+30 to T+60 days)

- Lessons-learned session with all parties involved.
- Update of TOM (technical / organizational) based on findings.
- Update of this incident plan if gaps were identified.
- Employee training on specific incident patterns.
- Complete incident register entry (lessons learned, preventive measures).

10. Incident Register (Art. 33(5) GDPR)

All breaches — reported or not — are documented in an internal register. Minimum entries:
- Incident number + date/time of detection.
- Description of the incident and affected data.
- Risk assessment (rationale).
- Measures taken.
- Notification to authority / data subjects (yes/no, with date).
- Time to remediation.
- Lessons learned.

Retention: 6 years.

11. Training and Tests

- All employees are informed about this incident plan at least annually.
- At least semi-annual tabletop exercise (theoretical incident walkthrough).
- For substantial system changes (e.g., new AVV partner, new payment method), update the plan.
